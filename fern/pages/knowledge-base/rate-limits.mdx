The Free plan includes 3 inboxes and 100 emails a day, with no separate monthly cap and no custom domains. Developer (\$20 a month) raises that to 10 inboxes, 10 domains, and 1,000 emails a day, and Startup (\$200 a month) to 150 inboxes, 150 domains, and 15,000 emails a day. When you reach a sending limit the API returns `429` with a `Retry-After` header; when you reach an inbox or domain limit it returns `403` with the code `limit_exceeded`.

## At a glance

<Markdown src="../../snippets/plan-limits.mdx" />

Each recipient counts as one email: a message with 2 addresses in `to` and 1 in `cc` uses 3. Inbound mail never counts. The [pricing page](https://www.agentmail.to/pricing) lists 3,000 emails a month for Free: that is 100 a day over a month, not a separate limit.

An account an agent creates for itself through [agent sign-up](https://docs.agentmail.to/agent-onboarding) starts smaller: 1 inbox, no custom domains, 10 emails a day, and it can email only the human attached to it. Verifying or claiming the account moves it to Free. See [Why can my agent only email its human?](https://docs.agentmail.to/knowledge-base/agent-sign-up-limits)

## How the sending windows reset

The daily, 5-minute, and 30-day limits are fixed windows, not rolling ones:

- The **5-minute** window starts on the clock's 5-minute marks (for example 12:00, 12:05, 12:10 UTC).
- The **daily** window resets at 00:00 UTC.
- The **30-day** window is a fixed 30-day period. It does not follow calendar months or your billing date.

The `Retry-After` header on a `429` gives the seconds until the window that blocked you resets. AgentMail emails your organization when it has used 80% of its 30-day limit.

## The new-account recipient limit

On the Free plan, a new account can email only a few new people at first. Paid plans do not have this limit, and upgrading removes it right away.

| Account | First hour | First 24 hours | First week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | 5 recipients | 10 recipients | 25 recipients |
| Free, after an agent account is verified or claimed | 3 recipients | 5 recipients | 10 recipients |

- The counts are distinct recipients, counted from when the account was created, or from verification for an agent account.
- Replies to people who have emailed your account do not count.
- Variants of one mailbox count once: `jo+test1@example.com` and `jo+test2@example.com` are the same recipient.
- A Free account that has not sent anything for 10 days or more starts the limit again on its next send.

Going over returns `429` with the code `rate_limit_exceeded` and a `message` that starts with "New account recipient limit exceeded" (or "Recipient limit exceeded" after a restart). The message says when the limit rises and when it lifts, and `Retry-After` gives the seconds until it rises.

## API request rate

AgentMail also limits how fast each organization can call the API, so one runaway client cannot slow the API down for everyone. The limits sit well above normal agent traffic, and higher plans get higher limits. Each pod and inbox gets a share of the organization's limit, so one busy inbox can be limited while the rest keep working.

## Errors when you reach a limit

| What you reached | Status and `code` | `message` | Extra fields in the body | Headers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A sending limit | `429` `rate_limit_exceeded` | `Daily send limit exceeded`, `Five minute send limit exceeded`, or `Monthly send limit exceeded` | `resource` (`send`), `window` (`five_minute`, `daily`, or `monthly`), `limit`, `upgrade_url` | `Retry-After`, `ratelimit-limit`, `ratelimit-reset` |
| The new-account recipient limit | `429` `rate_limit_exceeded` | Starts with "New account recipient limit exceeded" or "Recipient limit exceeded" | None | `Retry-After` |
| The API request rate | `429` `rate_limit_exceeded` | Varies | `resource` (`request`), `scope` (`organization`, `pod`, or `inbox`), `exceeded` | `Retry-After` |
| Your inbox or domain limit | `403` `limit_exceeded` | `Inbox limit exceeded` or `Domain limit exceeded` | `resource` (`inbox` or `domain`), `upgrade_url`, and `limit` when known | `x-agentmail-resource`, plus `x-agentmail-limit` and `x-agentmail-remaining` when the limit is known |

Every error body also has a `fix` field that says what to change. Other `limit_exceeded` errors, such as too many webhooks or labels, are fixed product limits: they have no `upgrade_url`, and a plan change does not raise them.

The SDKs retry a `429` twice by default, waiting for `Retry-After` up to a minute. That clears a request-rate `429`, but a daily or 30-day limit will not reset in time, so catch the error and check `window`:

<CodeBlocks>
```python title="Python"
import os

from agentmail import AgentMail
from agentmail.core.api_error import ApiError

client = AgentMail(api_key=os.environ["AGENTMAIL_API_KEY"])

try:
    client.inboxes.messages.send(
        "agent@agentmail.to", to=["customer@example.com"], subject="Your order shipped", text="It's on the way."
    )
except ApiError as error:
    body = error.body if isinstance(error.body, dict) else {}
    if error.status_code == 429:
        # window is five_minute, daily, or monthly for sending limits
        print("try again in", (error.headers or {}).get("retry-after"), "seconds:", body.get("window"), body.get("message"))
    elif error.status_code == 403 and body.get("code") == "limit_exceeded":
        print("plan limit reached:", body.get("resource"), body.get("upgrade_url"))
    else:
        raise
```

```typescript title="TypeScript"
import { AgentMailClient, AgentMailError } from "agentmail";

const client = new AgentMailClient({ apiKey: process.env.AGENTMAIL_API_KEY });

try {
    await client.inboxes.messages.send("agent@agentmail.to", {
        to: ["customer@example.com"],
        subject: "Your order shipped",
        text: "It's on the way.",
    });
} catch (error) {
    if (!(error instanceof AgentMailError)) throw error;
    const body = (error.body ?? {}) as { code?: string; message?: string; window?: string; resource?: string; upgrade_url?: string };
    if (error.statusCode === 429) {
        // window is five_minute, daily, or monthly for sending limits
        console.log("try again in", error.rawResponse?.headers.get("retry-after"), "seconds:", body.window, body.message);
    } else if (error.statusCode === 403 && body.code === "limit_exceeded") {
        console.log("plan limit reached:", body.resource, body.upgrade_url);
    } else {
        throw error;
    }
}
```

## Staying under the limits

- **Use webhooks or WebSockets instead of polling.** Listing an inbox in a tight loop is the most common way to reach the request rate. [Webhooks](https://docs.agentmail.to/webhooks-overview) and [WebSockets](https://docs.agentmail.to/websockets) tell you about new mail as it arrives.
- **Spread out batch jobs.** Pace a job that reads or sends a lot over a few minutes instead of firing every request at once.
- **Make retries safe.** Pass an `idempotency_key` when you send, so a retried request does not send the email twice. See [How do I prevent duplicate sends?](https://docs.agentmail.to/knowledge-base/preventing-duplicate-sends)
- **Watch your usage.** In the [AgentMail Console](https://console.agentmail.to), **Settings**, then **Usage** shows your inboxes, custom domains, and members against your plan's limits.

## Get help

To raise a limit, [upgrade your plan](https://console.agentmail.to/dashboard/upgrade) or add an add-on. For more than Startup offers, see Enterprise on the [pricing page](https://www.agentmail.to/pricing) or email [support@agentmail.cc](mailto:support@agentmail.cc). If you get a `429` or `403` that does not match your plan more than 5 minutes after a plan change, email support with your organization ID, the full error response, and the time of the request.
